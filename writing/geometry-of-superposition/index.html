The geometry that lets many things share one vector

Random directions in high dimensions are very nearly at right angles, which is the whole reason a single vector can hold many items and give any one of them back.

Mateo Hernandez · 10 August 2026

The idea that one list of numbers can hold many separate things at once sounds like it should not work. It works because of a fact about high-dimensional space that has no analogue in the three dimensions we picture.

M003 states it precisely. Draw points at random and, with high probability, every one is near unit length and every pair is near orthogonal. Both errors shrink as the dimension grows, and they grow only with the logarithm of how many points there are.

The number that matters#

At a thousand stored items in ten thousand dimensions, the bound on the inner product between any two of them is 0.064382199979161149. Raise the dimension a hundredfold, to a million, and the same bound falls to 0.0064379012978194954.

That is the capacity argument made numeric. A hundredfold rise in dimension buys a tenfold drop in interference, and the cost of storing one more item grows only logarithmically.

The part that is not settled#

A bound on each pair is not a bound on their sum. Whether the interference from many stored items cancels or accumulates is a separate question, and the two answers lead to designs that differ by orders of magnitude in what they need.
