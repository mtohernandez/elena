A 1952 model that still holds

The Hodgkin and Huxley description of the nerve impulse is old enough to predate the transistor and still predicts a measured conduction velocity to three digits.

Mateo Hernandez · 11 August 2026

About this article

Published
11 August 2026

S001 is the oldest source in this corpus and among the most exact. Hodgkin and Huxley described the current across a nerve membrane as a set of coupled differential equations, fitted them to measurements from a squid axon, and then used them to predict something they had not fitted.

The prediction#

From the fitted constants, their model gives a conduction velocity of 18.7605337642 metres per second. The measured figure they report is 18.8. That is agreement to the precision they printed.

A model that reproduces the data it was fitted to is unremarkable. A model that produces a number it was never shown is doing something else.

Why it is here#

Not as a blueprint. Biology is evidence about what is possible in a physical system, not a design to be traced. What this source establishes is a standard: a quantitative claim about a mechanism, stated as an equation, checkable by anyone who cares to run it.

Sources cited
