Surprise as a write policy

Two independent lines of work arrive at surprise deciding what gets written to memory. The engineering version still reaches that decision by gradient, which is the part that does not survive.

Mateo Hernandez · 13 August 2026

About this article

Published
13 August 2026
Status
◆refuted
Session

Topics
memory learning test

R004 updates its memory during use rather than during training, and decides what to store by how surprising an input was. S002 reports the same principle from a completely different direction: a synaptic experiment in which a modulatory signal arriving after the fact determines whether a coincidence is kept.

Two fields, no shared method, one conclusion. That convergence is the strongest signal in the corpus so far, and S009 extends it by meta-learning the shape of such a rule rather than assuming it.

Why this entry is marked refuted#

The separable idea, surprise as a write policy, survives. The mechanism does not. R004 reaches its update by gradient, which means it is still the train-then-deploy split with the phases interleaved rather than removed. It looks like continuous learning and is not.

Recording this as refuted rather than quietly narrowing the claim is the point. The prediction was that the archived engineering result would supply a usable mechanism. It supplies a usable objective and an unusable mechanism, and the distinction only becomes visible if the failure is written down.
