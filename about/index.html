About

Why this project exists, what it is for, and how to read what gets published here.

Mateo Hernandez·Last updated 13 August 2026

Why this exists

I do not expect anything.

I do not expect to make a discovery, either something to happen or emerge.

What I am trying to do is push back against a long stretch of hard times.

There is an idea called the "immortality project": people work toward something greater than themselves. A legacy. Most people give a great deal to it.

Wars and large conflicts tend to begin where those projects overlap, or where one gets in the way of another.

I could not care less. And you should not either.

Elena is a self-learning life form. I do not expect to build her, and I do not expect to find her. If the project turns into something, we will have made progress. If nothing is found, then nothing was expected in the first place.

The work rests on the foundations of artificial intelligence, but I will not lean on the term. I have heard it so often that I now hate it.

I intend to run Elena and give her to everyone, for free. I am not expecting compensation from this, and I am not expecting support either.

She will be trained, and I mean that word in its ordinary sense: learning how to do something the same a human baby learns to talk or walk.

Elena is not here to replace us. She is here to make us smarter and to let us take in knowledge faster, without reaching into the brain and without altering who the person using her is.

The thing I want to mitigate, and I know this is selfish, is the absence of common sense that falls into stupidity. Everyone will remain ignorant of something; that is a law of the universe. But there could at least be a floor. Enough of one to avoid the world as it stands in 2026, which I expect to keep getting worse for a while. Many bad things will still happen before any of this has an effect on anything.

None of this treats religion, identity or culture as bad. What I want is for newer generations to hold these things beneath them rather than be held by them. Conscious enough to live above all of it and move forward with something new, without forgetting the past and with their history straight. Not searching for an absolute truth. Just reaching conclusions by common sense, that make sense. Understanding themselves. Seeing the actions they are taking as they take them. Understanding the world around them, and being driven less by feeling and more by logic wherever that is possible. It is hard, because it runs against our nature, but logic is what keeps a person from acting without thinking and doing damage that cannot be undone.

I think this project will open a gap. But only because some people will choose not to use it. And that's okay.

This is not a large language model, and it is not another model.

I do not have a word for what it is yet.

Where this started (just a bit of a story)

In 2020 I discussed with my mother about making an artificial intelligence that could help my grandmother with dementia get through the ordinary parts of a day. I was young, and naive enough to believe I could build it then.

I intend to do the work now, and the idea has also outgrown where it began. It is now about putting knowledge in reach of anyone who wants it.

How to read what is here

Every entry carries a status. Open means the question is live. Supported means a prediction held and the observation that would have killed it was given a real chance to fire. Refuted means it fired. Inconclusive means the test could not decide.

Refuted entries are kept in full and are listed on the same footing.

The graph

Entries reference each other, and they reference the papers and books they rest on. Those references are drawn as a map, so it is possible to follow how one result led to the next instead of reading everything in order. Articles are circles. Sources are diamonds. The same information is on that page as a table for anyone who would rather read it, or who is using a screen reader.

Sources

Every paper and book cited here is listed by reference only: title, author, year and a link to the original at its own publisher. Nothing from them is reproduced on this site.

Accessibility

The site aims at WCAG 2.2 AA. Colour contrast is recomputed from the specification's own formula. The typeface is Atkinson Hyperlegible, drawn by the Braille Institute for readers with low vision.

Under Text and theme in the header there are controls for theme, text size, line spacing, line width and typeface, including your own system serif or sans if you prefer one.

What this site does not do

No cookies. No analytics. No third-party requests of any kind.

To follow the work, use the feed.
